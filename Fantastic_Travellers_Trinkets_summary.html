🧳 EXPLANATION

This poem is a portrait of nostalgia and longing, framed through the life of a traveller. The bells and chimes evoke echoes of journeys, sounds that carry memory across time and space. The traveller, once free to wander, now finds himself confined, his body weakened and his mobility reduced, symbolized by the wish for a wheelchair. The contrast between past freedom and present limitation deepens the sense of melancholy. His old suit, coats, and boots become relics of adventure, souvenirs of a lifetime’s stroll that he still polishes with care. The collection of trinkets — sand from deserts, miniatures from sculptors, berries from tribes, and a mercury hourglass — represents the grandeur of his experiences, each item a fragment of the world he once explored. Yet, the poem emphasizes that even in physical stillness, the traveller’s mind continues to roam. Maps and books become his new landscapes, allowing imagination to sustain the spirit of adventure. The deeper meaning lies in the persistence of longing and the resilience of memory, showing how journeys live on even when the body cannot follow. Ultimately, the poem celebrates the grandeur of a wandering soul, whose treasures are not only objects but the enduring desire to explore.
