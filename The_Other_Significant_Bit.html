🔀 THE OTHER SIGNIFICANT BIT

Across the pavement I was strolling past,
There stood a divergence quite unusual to see,
I came to a halt and the breeze blew fast;
I frowned at the rather peculiar scene.

Of the two paths, both foresting in deep,
One was a merrily evergreen lane
And the other had thorny trees in the bleak,
Both separated by a treeless plane.

I saw a man walking past the woods,
Triumphant and well-dressed, how happy he stood;
Staring at the 'rather peculiar scene',
Said, "How beautiful is the spreading green,
Which would cover the thorns too, oh so soon
Just like I got my job this noon!"
He walked away, happier than ever,
Higher than the ninth cloud in the sky,
Followed by a woman whose cloud seemed to have never,
Ever reached upward that high.

She too, well-dressed but not triumphant,
Walked past and halted at the 'quite unusual scene',
Staring at the thorny forest she exclaimed,
"How pity the nature is dying so quick!
All those leaves on the other green side
Would slowly and steadily end their life,
And as the last leaf finally perishes,
So shall I... as it happened today!"
And she walked away with a sigh.

I, so neutral, just stood and thought,
How miraculous is the mind of ours,
Noticing only what favours it,
Ignoring the other significant bit.
